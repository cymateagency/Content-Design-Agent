# Trends and timely hooks

Trend-led posts work when the reference is instantly recognisable and the agency-culture truth lands in the same glance.

## How often
About once every 3–4 posts (and whenever asked), research what the whole world is talking about right now and build the hook from it. Check the content log: if none of the last 3–4 posts was trend-led, this one should be. Mark trend-led posts in the content log (`trend: <what>` in the Layout column) so the cadence is easy to check. The rest stay evergreen agency truths (`funnel.md`).

## How to pick a trend
1. Search what's trending this week (social formats, memes, cultural moments, upcoming holidays). Prefer things an agency founder or creative director would recognise.
2. Check it's still current (last 2–3 weeks), not stale or controversial.
3. Find the bridge to agency life and recognition. If the bridge takes a sentence to explain, pick another trend.
4. Say which trend you used and why in the delivery note.

## Evergreen formats that fit AWA
POV · red flag or green flag · put a finger down · this or that · expectation vs reality · starter pack · tell me without telling me · report card · unpopular opinion · "if you know, you know" · year-in-review.

## Calendar hooks (check dates each year)
New Year and planning season · Monday energy · performance-review season · Employee Appreciation Day · Mental Health Awareness Month · summer slowdown and holidays · Q4 crunch and holiday parties · year-end recognition.
The AWA award cycle (nominations open, deadline, winner reveal) is a hook too, but its dates always come from the request.

## Pop culture without borrowing someone else's IP
Reference a show, film or viral moment through the hook, the wording and the format, with original visuals. Don't recreate characters, stills, posters, logos or celebrity photos, and don't imply an endorsement.

## Sensitivity
Skip tragedies, politics, religion and anything mocking people. Layoffs and burnout are real for the audience: be warm, never flippant. Holiday posts honour the occasion first.
