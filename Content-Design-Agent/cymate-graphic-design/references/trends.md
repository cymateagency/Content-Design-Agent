# Trends and timely hooks

Trend-led posts get the most traction when the reference is instantly recognisable and the outbound lesson lands in the same glance.

## How often to go global
About once every 3–4 posts (and whenever asked), research what the **whole world** is talking about right now: something almost anyone recognises regardless of industry or country (a huge film or series release, a global sporting event, a viral meme or sound, a major tech launch, a worldwide holiday or moment). Check the content log: if none of the last 3–4 posts was trend-led, this one should be. The rest of the time, evergreen formats and GTM topics are fine.

## How to pick a trend
1. When the user asks for something trending, no topic is given, or the cadence above says it's time, run a web search for what’s trending this week (social trends, memes, viral formats, big cultural moments, upcoming holidays and events). Prefer things a B2B founder or sales leader would recognise.
2. Check the trend is still current (last 2–3 weeks), not already stale or controversial.
3. Find the bridge: the moment where the trend and an outbound truth overlap. If the bridge takes a sentence to explain, pick another trend.
4. Say which trend you used and why in the delivery note, so the team can sanity-check timing.

## Evergreen formats that keep working
POV · red flag or green flag · put a finger down · this or that · expectation vs reality · nobody vs me · starter pack · tell me without telling me · ways to say I love you · rate this /10 · unpopular opinion · "if you know, you know" · year-in-review wrapped · countdowns.

## Calendar hooks (check dates each year)
New Year and Q1 planning · Valentine’s Day · Q2/Q3/Q4 kickoffs · US holidays (Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Black Friday, Cyber Monday) · Halloween · year-end wrapped season (December) · big sporting or tech events.

## Pop culture without borrowing someone else’s IP
Reference a movie, show, game or viral moment through the **hook, the wording and the format**, with original visuals: a "brand new day" headline over a city skyline, web line-art, a ticket stub, a trailer-style title card. Don’t recreate or paste characters, movie stills, posters, logos, or photos of celebrities, and don’t imply an endorsement. The idea should be recognisable from the words and the vibe.

## Sensitivity
Skip tragedies, politics, religion and anything mocking people. Holiday posts honour the occasion first and tie to the work lightly (like the Labor Day post).
