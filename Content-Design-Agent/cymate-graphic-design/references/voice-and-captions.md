# Voice and captions

## Cymate voice
The writing should feel professional, sharp and clear, with a little fun: something an enterprise buyer would trust, written by people who clearly know outbound. Show expertise through specifics (the mechanism, the tactic, the right term), not claims about being experts.
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Plain words.
- **No em dashes** anywhere (captions or on-image text). Use a full stop or comma instead.
- No filler or hype adjectives ("game-changing", "revolutionary", "insane", "unlock"), no throat-clearing.
- Don't repeat a point for emphasis.
- Confident and specific; fun is allowed when the topic is light (holidays, culture posts), but never at the expense of clarity.

## Facts and numbers
Use only numbers, results and client names that appear in the brief, the source content the user gave (transcript, draft, doc), or that the user confirms. Never invent a statistic, reply rate, client result or quote. If a design needs one you don't have, put a bracketed placeholder (`[X]%`) and flag it in the delivery note.

## The four-part caption structure
Used for LinkedIn and Instagram feed posts and carousels. Never show the part labels in the final caption.
1. **Problem** (1–3 short sentences): name the pain or false assumption. This is the hook, so the first line must work alone before "…more".
2. **Reframe** (the bulk): why the problem exists or what actually works. Proof points and the mechanism go here.
3. **Soft CTA**: a reflective question about the reader's own situation. No sales language. ("How confident are you in your list right now?")
4. **Hard CTA**: the explicit next step with placement: link in bio, link in comments, or booking link. Confirm which CTA is live for this post; if the user hasn't said, ask or leave `[CTA: link in bio / comments / booking]`.

Hashtags go at the very end on their own line.

## Platform versions
- **LinkedIn**: ~600–1,300 characters, line breaks between short paragraphs, 3–5 hashtags. Links go in comments unless told otherwise. Hard links: cymate.io, thecoldacademy.com.
- **Instagram**: shorter (~300–700 characters), more conversational, "link in bio", 5–10 hashtags. Handle: @cymate.io.
- When the user doesn't name a platform, give both versions.

## Engagement posts
For light posts (holidays, culture, memes) the four-part structure can be dropped for a single line built to get comments ("Which one are you?"). Ask if unsure which the user wants.
